Naklua: the Fishing Quarter and Its Seafood Market

Naklua — นาเกลือ, Na Kluea — is the fishing and trading quarter at the top of Pattaya bay, on the same shore as the beaches but not a beach itself. What it has instead is a market: Lan Pho, one of the two fresh markets Pattaya City runs, where the catch is sold by weight and the stalls beside it will steam, boil or grill what you buy for 20–40 THB a kilogram.

It suits people who will get up for a market and eat their fish a few steps from where it was landed. It is the wrong end of the bay for anyone who wants the sea at the foot of the hotel path — that is the beach next door, on the headland to the south, or the northern stretch of Pattaya Beach beyond it.

What Naklua is, and what the beach is not

DASTA, the government agency behind the Old Town Na Kluea plan, describes the subdistrict as a small old community inside Pattaya with an old market, a seafood market, an old Thai–Chinese community, temples and shrines, traditional fishing, cycling round the last piece of mangrove forest in Pattaya, bird watching and street art — and it treats Lan Pho as the link between the fishing communities and that mangrove.

That is the character of the quarter, and it is not the character of the beach. Wong Amat is the bay's swimming shore, and the northern stretch of Pattaya Beach is sand with a road behind it. Naklua's frontage on the water is a working one: the fishing pier at 12.9761, 100.8965 is where the boats land the catch the market sells, and the park, the market and the pier sit about a kilometre apart at the road's north end. Swimmers take the headland; this shore is the one you walk round.

Buying at the market

Lan Pho is in two halves — a general market and the fresh seafood market — and it is one of the two fresh markets Pattaya City runs. The seafood side works on a morning: the stalls are fullest early, the pick is best before the day has warmed, and the price moves with the catch, so ask the price per kilogram at the stall before you choose.

Then carry the bag to the cooking stalls, which charge 20–40 THB a kilogram to steam, boil or grill it and sell the dipping sauce separately. That is the meal: market weight plus a cooking charge, eaten at a plastic table a few steps from the ice boxes it came out of.

The other market in Naklua

New Naklua Market — ตลาดใหม่นาเกลือ — on Sawang Fa road is a different market with different hours: a morning session from 05:00 to 10:00 for fresh seafood, meat, vegetables and fruit, and an evening session from about 15:00 to 20:00 for prepared food, sweets, snacks and fruit. Where Lan Pho is the fish, this one is the rest of the shopping: meat, vegetables, fruit and something cooked for the evening.

Eating fish in the quarter

Moom Aroi stands at the end of Pattaya–Naklua Road, after Lan Pho market and behind Bang Lamung police station, with about a hundred tables and its frontage on the water. It opens daily from 11:00 to 23:00, and it is the sit-down version of the market meal: the same fish, the cooking done for you, and a table rather than a plastic stool.

The cheaper way to eat is the market's own cooking service, at 20–40 THB a kilogram over the price of the fish. If you want one meal in Naklua to be the whole point of the trip up here, buy at the market in the morning and eat lunch at the stalls.

Getting to Naklua

Songthaews run north along Naklua Road past the Dolphin Roundabout at 15–20 THB a trip, distance-dependent, since the fare rose on 10 April 2026, and none of them runs along the beach itself: you ride to the north end of the road and walk the last stretch to the park and the market.
